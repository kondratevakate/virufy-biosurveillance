Why

THE GAP

Public health teams learn about a respiratory wave from tests and doctor visits, days after people fall ill. Wastewater shows it earlier for a whole city, but not for a hall, a worksite or an arrivals gate.

THE BORDER

In our check of 17 public monitors in the EU, none publishes airport wastewater or air sampling. The Dutch RIVM states that Schiphol wastewater data are not published.

How it works

1 · SITE

A listening device at the site, or a phone for a spot check, counts coughs with an on-device sound model and forgets the audio within a second. Only counts per 15 minutes, the kind of place and a 1 km map cell are shared, with an aggregator run under the health authority.

2 · ZONE

Each zone is compared with its own weekday baseline, adjusted for how many people are listening. Zones with fewer than 10 participants are hidden.

3 · ALERT

A running sum of coughs above the baseline (a set of Poisson CUSUM charts) raises one alert for a sustained rise, set for about one false alarm in 180 days, with the reason and the responsible role.

4 · BORDER SIGNAL

Aircraft wastewater series sit next to the zones as a second, independent signal of what is arriving.

Infectious-disease surveillance in the UAE

The UAE measured what Europe still does not publish. Dubai tested wastewater from arriving aircraft in 2020 (Albastaki et al.), and a national programme sampled 453 sites for two years, while most wastewater studies elsewhere were short and small (Wadi et al.).

THE UAE DECIDES WHAT LEAVES THE NETWORK

  • Phones send counts only. Audio never leaves the phone.
  • In a pilot, counts go to an aggregator hosted in the UAE and run under the health authority, not by Virufy.
  • The authority chooses which zones are watched, how often counts arrive, and what, if anything, is published.
  • Nothing is shared in real time with anyone outside unless the authority turns it on.

WHAT IT ADDS

The studies on the map show that wastewater worked in the UAE, but none of them is a running signal, and wastewater covers a city, not a hall. A daily count of coughs per venue, held by the authority, adds a signal for single arrivals halls, worksites and clinics, next to the wastewater it already trusts.

We found no public, regularly updated UAE surveillance data, which fits this model: the data stay with the authority. MOHAP is federal and is not tied to one emirate on the map.

Our research

IN PREPARATION

Detecting outbreaks from cough counts: a load-adjusted Poisson model

Daily cough counts are modelled as Poisson(listening time × (share of people ill × cough rate + detector false alarms)). A bank of Poisson CUSUMs, calibrated to one false alarm per 180 days, is compared with EARS C2 and a median/MAD rule; detection delay is set against its Kullback-Leibler lower bound, and the count stream against airport wastewater. In simulation, a 50% rise is caught in 1.5 days at 200 listening hours a day.

UAE: wastewater and the airport 4

What the UAE has already measured. Wastewater tracked clinical cases across the country and peaked before them, and aircraft arriving at Dubai Airport carried the virus. None of it is a running public data source; cough counts would add a signal for single venues.

  • PROJECT · UAE PREVENT-DXB: sequencing of surfaces, air and wastewater at Dubai International Airport, funded by a Dubai RDI grant Press release · University of Birmingham Dubai, Mohammed Bin Rashid University, Dubai Police
  • 2023 · UAE 16,858 wastewater samples from 453 UAE locations: viral counts peaked about one to two weeks before clinical cases Science of the Total Environment · Khalifa University
  • 2021 · UAE SARS-CoV-2 found in wastewater of 13.6% of 198 aircraft arriving at Dubai Airport Science of the Total Environment · Dubai Police
  • 2021 · UAE SARS-CoV-2 RNA in 85% of untreated wastewater samples from 38 UAE sites, none in treated effluent Science of the Total Environment · Khalifa University
