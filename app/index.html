Why

THE GAP

Public health teams learn about a respiratory wave from tests and doctor visits, days after people fall ill. Wastewater shows it earlier for a whole city, but not for a hall, a worksite or an arrivals gate.

THE BORDER

In our check of 17 public monitors in the EU, none publishes airport wastewater or air sampling. The Dutch RIVM states that Schiphol wastewater data are not published.

How it works

1 · PHONE

A phone counts coughs with an on-device sound model and forgets the audio within a second. Only counts per 15 minutes, the kind of place and a 1 km map cell are shared.

2 · ZONE

Each zone is compared with its own weekday baseline, adjusted for how many people are listening. Zones with fewer than 10 participants are hidden.

3 · ALERT

A running sum of coughs above the baseline (a set of Poisson CUSUM charts) raises one alert for a sustained rise, set for about one false alarm in 180 days, with the reason and the responsible role.

4 · BORDER SIGNAL

Aircraft wastewater series sit next to the zones as a second, independent signal of what is arriving.
